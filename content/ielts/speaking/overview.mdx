## Overview

| Feature | Description |
| --- | --- |
| Duration | 11–14 minutes |
| Number of parts | 3 |
| Type | Face-to-face interview with examiner |
| Skills assessed | Fluency, coherence, vocabulary, grammar, pronunciation |

## Scoring criteria (each 25%)

| Criterion | What it means |
| --- | --- |
| Fluency and Coherence | Smooth flow of ideas, logical structure, minimal hesitation |
| Lexical Resource | Range of vocabulary, appropriate word choices |
| Grammatical Range & Accuracy | Variety of grammar, accurate use of tenses and structures |
| Pronunciation | Clear speech, intonation, rhythm, ability to be understood |

## Strategy tips

| Tip type | What to do |
| --- | --- |
| Fluency | Don't aim for perfect grammar. Keep talking confidently. |
| Vocabulary | Use topic-related words and paraphrasing. |
| Grammar | Mix tenses, use conditionals and modals. |
| Pronunciation | Stress key words, vary intonation. |
| Avoid | One-word answers, memorized templates, long pauses |

## Useful phrases by function

| Function | Example phrases |
| --- | --- |
| Starting | Well, to be honest… / Actually… |
| Explaining | This is because… / One reason might be… |
| Giving examples | For instance… / Let me give you an example… |
| Expressing opinion | I believe that… / In my view… |
| Speculating | It could be that… / Perhaps it's because… |
| Comparing | Compared to… / In contrast… |

<Callout>
### You don't have to tell the truth

You're not being judged on honesty, you're being judged on your English.

If the examiner asks "Do you enjoy cooking?" and you actually hate it but
can't think of how to explain that, it's okay to say you love cooking and go
on about your "passion" for making pasta. IELTS Speaking isn't a lie detector
test — they won't fact-check your stories. Your grammar, vocabulary, fluency
and pronunciation are what matter most.

So if making up a story helps you speak longer, smoother, and with better
language, do it. Just be consistent, be confident, and show off your English.
</Callout>

## Preparation strategy

*From the [IELTS Speaking Preparation Strategy](https://app.notion.com/p/34ff75584dea83e480dc01205d83ca1d) sub-page.*

### Build daily speaking habits

- **Record yourself daily** (e.g. answering 3 Part 1 questions).
- **Shadow native speakers** — copy their pace, stress, rhythm.
- **Use IELTS cue cards** to practise storytelling.
- **Speak in front of a mirror or with a study buddy.**

### Collect and practise with real questions

Reliable sources for topics: official Cambridge books, IELTS Liz / IELTS
Simon, YouTube mock tests.

### Learn vocabulary by topic

Create sheets of useful phrases per topic:

- **Education** — "lifelong learning", "academic pressure"
- **Environment** — "carbon footprint", "sustainable practices"
- **Hometown** — "close-knit community", "urban sprawl"
- **Work** — "work-life balance", "career advancement"

Aim to use 3–4 advanced vocab items naturally in every answer.

### Get feedback

Self-check, or ask a teacher/tutor to look for:

- Filler words ("umm", "like") overuse
- Repetition of basic vocab ("good", "bad", "nice")
- Lack of variety in sentence structure

Useful apps: Elsa Speak (pronunciation), Google Recorder for transcripts,
ChatGPT to simulate a mock test.

### Simulate full tests weekly

At least once a week: do a full mock speaking test, record all parts in one
go, time yourself strictly, then review and analyse — what went well, what
felt awkward.

<Callout>
### Bonus tips

- **Don't memorise full answers.** Memorise useful phrases and plug them into
  answers naturally.
- **Be expressive.** Add emotion and intonation — sound interested in what
  you're saying.
- **Be honest or lie — just sound fluent.** If you've never done something,
  make it up and focus on how you say it.
</Callout>
